# AWS certifications

`/aprender/certificaciones/` helps visitors compare the complete reviewed AWS certification offer and find preparation. The map groups credentials by their official categories, explains their intended profile, distinguishes exam versions and availability states, and links to AWS for every credential. It includes credentials with no locally selected materials. Beta and announced updates are not presented as general availability. Language-specific transitions remain explicit. Business is a category, not a mandatory step in a sequence.

The map links to three preparation pages:

- `/aprender/certificaciones/cloud-practitioner/`
- `/aprender/certificaciones/solutions-architect-associate/`
- `/aprender/certificaciones/ai-practitioner/`

Each page has a distinct introduction, exam facts, weighted domains, a practical study sequence, official preparation, reviewed selections, its own FAQ and dated sources. Recommended experience is separate from eligibility requirements. Selection notes identify purpose, scope and limits; no selection, practice score or study schedule guarantees passing. Official preparation and useful guidance remain available if catalog selections disappear.

The general FAQ addresses choosing a credential, categories, prerequisite versus recommendation, certification versus course certificates and microcredentials, versions/beta/retirement, language/access, preparation, accounts, prices, booking, results/retries, changes to bookings, validity and renewal, and suggesting corrections. The three exam FAQs address the corresponding preparation and comparison questions. Answers and links are visible in static HTML. They do not require JavaScript or expansion and do not emit `FAQPage` markup.

## Editorial ownership

`src/lib/certifications.mjs` owns the credential inventory, checked AWS facts and sources, page metadata, study guidance, FAQ and selection context. It is the common registry for map/detail destinations, search and sitemap integrations. AWS remains the external authority for its exams. The public review date means a recorded editorial check; it is not generated by the build and does not promise that a destination has not changed since.

DynamoDB owns each resource's current public title, description, URL, publication and relationships. A selected resource is referenced by stable ID with its purpose, scope, exam version, review date and the URL inspected during review. The resolver uses the currently published title and destination. It omits an absent resource or one no longer typed as content. When the published URL differs from the reviewed URL, it omits the selection and its note until an editor checks the new destination. The resource may still appear in the ordinary library. This does not detect changed content at the same URL.

Blog selections reference the existing article slug and take its title and destination from the article owner. A missing article, malformed local reference or duplicate preparation route is a configuration error. It must fail validation/build; it is not treated as a legitimate resource withdrawal. Catalog read failures retain the site's existing publication failure behavior; an empty fixture or catalog is never a fallback.

The FAQs use official sources and general editorial guidance, without named catalog examples or claims based on selection counts. Removing a selection therefore removes only its own card/context and leaves no resource-dependent FAQ links. The ordinary library retains its historical groups and all filtering, ordering, pagination, history and hash-link behavior.

## Maintaining the content

1. Check the official inventory and the affected exam guide/product page. Record the actual review date and sources; check category, code, language, beta/update/retirement scope and relevant dates. If sources disagree, publish only the supported information and explain the limit rather than choosing an unsupported exact date.
2. Update shared exam facts once and use them for summaries and FAQ answers. Review related preparation guidance when domains, policies or renewal options change. Keep credential URLs stable across exam-code changes.
3. Before adding or renewing a selection, inspect enough of the material to support the stated purpose, version, language, access and scope. Do not infer full coverage from a title, tag, README or host. Preserve explicit limits. A resource's directory addition date is not a content review.
4. For a catalog selection, confirm the published ID and actual URL, then record that URL as review evidence. For a blog selection, check the article and use its slug. Do not maintain another resource title or outbound destination in Git.
5. Run the repository checks and generated-site verification with the intended build mode. Changes publish through the normal Git build and current catalog snapshot. Catalog withdrawal follows the existing publication process and does not erase cached HTML immediately.

No new CMS, catalog schema, IAM permission, automated curator or freshness deadline is introduced.

## Discovery and indexing

Home and Aprender keep their existing link to the overview. Each supported credential row links to its preparation page. Contextual links in the five relevant blog articles provide the next useful destination for readers already studying; the global menu remains concise. Preparation pages link back to the map and their existing library group, to official sources, and to useful related articles or exam comparisons. Cloud Practitioner links to both its study plan and its Spanish resource guide: the plan organizes study and readiness, while the guide helps choose courses and community recordings with their stated limits.

The four pages have distinct titles, descriptions and H1s, self canonicals and the normal site social metadata. They are indexable in production, excluded in preview, and represented once in the sitemap. Review and build dates are not sitemap `lastmod`. Preparation pages are not blog articles and do not receive `BlogPosting`. Search includes exam codes, purpose and the visible FAQ even when no catalog records mention the exam. Neither these technical checks nor FAQ content establishes indexing, rich results, ranking or traffic improvement.
